I hope you don't mind a slightly pedantic email out of the blue. I’m a postdoctoral researcher at King’s College and the LSE just joining a research project on the fertility transition in 19th-century France (FaMiGrowth, ANR) at the University of Aix-Marseille, and I've been using your Guerry package as a reference shapefile — really lovely resource, thank you for maintaining it.
While cross-checking your gfrance polygons against a couple of period-accurate alternatives — Cédric Chambru's Department Boundaries in France, c. 1790 (Harvard Dataverse, doi:10.7910/DVN/HJISNR) and Victor Gay's TRF-GIS at 1870 (doi:10.7910/DVN/ULQYM5) — I think I've found two related artefacts in the eastern frontier that may be worth flagging. Both come from the same root cause: the package's geometries appear to be modern French départements with only the 1968 Paris splits, and Savoie, Haute-Savoie and Alps Maritimes acquisitions manually undone, leaving the post-Franco-Prussian-War reshuffle of Alsace-Lorraine intact.
The one is Meurthe and Moselle. In the 1830 that Guerry was writing about, these were two separate départements with a particular internal boundary; after France lost most of Alsace-Lorraine to Germany in 1871, the western remnants were merged into the new Meurthe-et-Moselle and the post-1918 Moselle was reconstituted on a slightly different line. The gfrance geometries carry the post-1918 split rather than the pre-1871 one — the combined region is right but the internal boundary is off by something like forty per cent of either département's area, with the offset running right through what was Guerry's industrialising eastern Lorraine. The same root cause means Haut-Rhin in your shapefile is also the modern version, with Territoire de Belfort missing (that département was carved out of Haut-Rhin in 1922, so the 1830 Haut-Rhin should still include it).
I attach a small image that I hope makes the issue clear at a glance.
I mention it because the package is very useful as a quick reference for historical French maps — your Guerry data ended up being one of the first things I tried for our study — and I'd worry that anyone doing point-in-polygon assignment of nineteenth-century coordinates against gfrance will silently misclassify a non-trivial number of Lorraine and Belfort records without ever noticing. The data fields themselves (Pop1831, Crime_pers and so on) look entirely correct — Pop1831 sums to the right 32.56 M of the 1831 census — so this seems purely on the geometry side.
